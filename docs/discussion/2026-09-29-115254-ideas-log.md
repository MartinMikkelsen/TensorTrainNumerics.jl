## Evidence and research directions

Endo and Takahashi, *Direct Construction of Quantized Tensor Trains With Small Rank for Laplace- or Gaussian-Transformed Analytic Functions*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.70391. Local PDF: 22 pages. Read the main development, numerical experiments, conclusion, and relevant Gaussian lemmas; visually checked theorem pages 4–5. This was not a complete proof audit.

Theorem 1 gives a directly constructible maximum QTT rank of soft-O(log(1/epsilon)) plus the number of exceptional grid points, under its transform-density analyticity and growth conditions. Theorem 2 gives soft-O(log(1/epsilon)^(9/4)) plus exceptions for Gaussian-transformed functions. Soft-O suppresses double-logarithmic factors. The mechanism is quadrature, rank-one exponentials, explicitly approximated Gaussians, and block sums of cores. The paper's two examples are 1/sqrt(1+x^2) and 1/(1+x^2), motivated by LCHS and Schrödingerization. Experiments use M=10, integer x in [0,2^M). Rounding gives maximum ranks 7 and 8 in those examples, not universal rank bounds. The authors identify higher-dimensional functions and improved constructive Gaussian ranks as future directions.

Application possibilities discussed in the forthcoming response:

- Fractional screened Poisson: directly assemble the inverse spectral multiplier (kappa^2+|k|^2)^(-alpha) and its QTT operator. The candidate contribution is quantization of all one-dimensional factors, explicit parameter-dependent rank/error bounds, and a meaningful operator or relative spectral error criterion.
- Coulomb and screened electrostatics: construct 3D interaction kernels and convolution operators. A serious extension would control singular-cell quadrature and forces/derivatives. Low-rank Coulomb and QTT Hartree/exchange calculations already exist.
- Spectral broadening: construct shifted Gaussian/Lorentzian kernel families for optical spectra. A contribution would need explicit dependence on width and peak count, stable derivatives or integrals, and a useful downstream application. This does not solve the problem of obtaining unknown eigenvalues or the full Green's function.

Preferred candidate, not a user-selected direction: on a periodic box [0,2*pi]^d with kappa>0 and 0<alpha<=1, solve (kappa^2-Delta)^alpha u=f. The multiplier admits the exact integral

    (kappa^2+|k|^2)^(-alpha)
      = 1/Gamma(alpha) * integral_0^infinity t^(alpha-1)
        exp(-kappa^2*t) product_j exp(-t*k_j^2) dt.

Quadrature plus direct Gaussian cores yields a candidate multiplier construction; diagonal lifting and a QTT Fourier transform yield a candidate solution operator. Signed frequencies, ordering, Gaussian errors, quadrature error, transform error, and optional rounding all require analysis. A rank bound for a sum of spatial tensor products does not by itself imply the same QTT rank in arbitrary bit ordering. Finite-difference eigenvalues involve sin^2, so this specific Gaussian formula is for the spectral discretization.

A useful research target is relative multiplier accuracy on the resolved frequency set. With an exact unitary Fourier transform, relative multiplier error <=epsilon implies relative solution error <=epsilon. For a positive approximant, it also yields a preconditioned condition-number bound (1+epsilon)/(1-epsilon). These implications are standard; the potential new theorem would be an explicit QTT construction achieving such accuracy with controlled ranks/cost. Approximate Fourier cores and rounding need their own error budget. Track mesh size, alpha, kappa, and bit ordering; do not assume grid-independent relative accuracy follows from the source paper's absolute-error result.

Experiments to assess viability: 2D/3D periodic problems with known Fourier solutions; mesh refinement and parameter sweeps; TT-cross, multiscale interpolation, and dense FFT baselines where feasible; construction time, application time, ranks before/after rounding, and actual solution errors. Any benefit depends on compressible right-hand sides and intermediate tensors.
